    How to use the lab
    1. Pick the kit, the scene and the water. The photograph is the camera’s view, rendered from the physics; it updates as you change anything.
    2. Drag the coloured strobe heads in the behind, top and side views. The behind view is what you see over your own shoulder, with the clock positions photographers use (10 and 2 o’clock). In the top and side views the small dot in front of each head aims it. With “Keep aim” on, a strobe keeps pointing at the subject (plus any angle you add) as you move it.
    3. The behind view also shows each beam’s footprint and the lens’s view on a slice of water: near the port, where their overlap (amber) makes backscatter, or at the subject, where the overlap shows coverage.
    4. The amber cloud in the diagrams shows where the light that reaches the lens as backscatter is scattered: it lives where the beams overlap the camera’s field of view. Shrink it and the haze and specks in the photograph go away.
    5. Switch the photograph to “Backscatter only” to see just the light scattered by the water, “Same scene in air” to see coverage and shadows without water, or “Exposure map” to read brightness in stops. “Enlarge” makes the photograph full width.
    6. The default water is a murky day in Plymouth Sound (about 3 m visibility), where placement makes a dramatic difference. For a lecture demonstration in clearer water, “Backscatter in the photograph” can show the scattered light 3 or 10 times stronger; the photograph then says so, and the numbers stay true to physics.
    7. The observer view shows the rig from outside, with the beams made visible by the water. Drag to turn around it, scroll or pinch to zoom, or pick 3/4, Side, Above or Behind; double-click resets. “See through the water” clears the water between you and the rig so the camera and beams are easy to see, while the beams themselves still use the real water.
    8. Save snapshots to compare placements; the numbers on the right explain each result.
